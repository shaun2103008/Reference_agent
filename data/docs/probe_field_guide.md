# Soil moisture probe — field guide

## What the probe measures

The capacitive probe reports a percentage derived from the dielectric constant of the surrounding soil. It does not measure water volume directly. Two soils at the same true water content can read several percentage points apart if their mineral composition differs, so a reading is only comparable against other readings from the same probe in the same bed.

## Calibration

Calibrate in two points before the first deployment of a season.

1. Hold the probe in still air, away from your hand, and record the reading. This is the dry reference. On a healthy probe it falls between 2 and 6 per cent.
2. Submerge the probe to the marked line in tap water and record the reading. This is the wet reference, normally between 88 and 96 per cent.

If the dry reference exceeds 10 per cent the probe body has absorbed moisture and must be dried for 24 hours before reuse. If the wet reference falls below 80 per cent, the sensing element has degraded and the probe should be replaced.

Recalibrate after any of the following: a new season, a probe that has been submerged past the marked line, or a reading that disagrees with a neighbouring probe by more than 8 percentage points.

## Installation depth

Install at root depth for the crop in question, not at the surface. For leafy vegetables this is 100 to 150 mm; for tomatoes and peppers, 200 to 300 mm. A probe installed too shallow tracks the weather rather than the plant, and will show a sharp spike after every light shower that never reaches the roots.

Backfill firmly. An air gap along the probe body is the single most common cause of readings that are implausibly low and unusually stable.

## Known failure modes

**Saline soil.** Dissolved salts raise the apparent dielectric constant, so a saline bed reads wetter than it is. Readings drift upward over a season as fertiliser salts accumulate. Flush the bed or relocate the probe.

**Direct sun on the cable.** UV degradation of the cable sheath admits moisture to the conductors, producing intermittent spikes to 99 per cent. Route cable under mulch.

**Freezing.** Below 0 degrees the water in the soil changes phase and the probe reads near-dry regardless of actual content. Readings taken below 2 degrees should be discarded, not corrected.

## Expected daily pattern

A correctly installed probe in an irrigated bed shows a sawtooth: a sharp rise within fifteen minutes of irrigation, then a slow decline of 2 to 5 percentage points per day depending on temperature and canopy cover. A flat line over several days means either no irrigation has occurred or the probe has lost soil contact.
